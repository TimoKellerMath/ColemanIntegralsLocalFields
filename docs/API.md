## isrational.m

```text
isrational.m -- recognise a plane model with rational coefficients.

If the model Q has coefficients in Q, coleman_data takes its "rational branch": the
Frobenius lift and the reduction of the pullbacks are computed over Q_p, which is much
cheaper than over the completion K = L_P, and only the results are moved to K.

Notation and conventions: see "Conventions and notation" in README.md.
```

Other functions of `isrational.m`:

* `coefficients_over_Q(Q)` -- The model Q as a polynomial over Q, if all its coefficients are rational.

## auxpolys.m

```text
auxpolys.m -- the auxiliary polynomials of a plane model, and the good-reduction tests.

For a plane model Q(x,y) = 0 of the curve X over a number field L, monic in y of degree d
(article: section "Good models", subsection "Plane models (Q,W^0,W^inf)"):
  Delta  the discriminant of Q with respect to y (up to a factor, see auxpolys),
  r      the squarefree polynomial with the same zeros and leading coefficient as Delta,
  s      a polynomial with s*dQ/dy = Delta modulo Q; it exists by the lemma that dQ/dy is
         invertible in O_K[x,1/r,y]/(Q).
The remaining functions test the hypotheses of the definition "Good-reduction assumptions"
for the triple (Q,W^0,W^inf) at a prime P. P is a prime ideal of O_L, or a rational prime p
in the rational branch of coleman_data.

Notation and conventions: see "Conventions and notation" in README.md.
```

Other functions of `auxpolys.m`:

* `auxpolys(Q)` -- The auxiliary polynomials r, Delta, s of the plane model Q.
* `genus_of_reduction(Q, P)` -- The genus g of X, computed from the reduction of the plane model modulo P.
* `is_separable_mod_P(f, P)` -- Whether f mod P has the same degree as f and is separable, i.e. the leading coefficient of f is a unit at P and the reduction of f has distinct roots.
* `is_integral(A, P)` -- Whether the matrix A over L(x) is P-integral in the sense of the good-reduction assumptions, used for W^0, W^inf and their inverses.

## frobenius.m

```text
frobenius.m -- the Frobenius lift on functions and its pullback of differential forms.

Article: section "Computing a Frobenius lift": the tau-semilinear lift F_tau^* with
F_tau^*(x) = x^(q_tau), F_tau^*(1/r) = 1/r^tau(x^(q_tau)) and F_tau^*(y) = beta, the root of
Q^tau(x^(q_tau),beta) = 0 congruent to y^(q_tau) (the proposition on the expression of the
Frobenius lift), computed by the algorithm "Compute matrix of Frobenius on functions"
(froblift), and the pullback F_tau^*(omega) of a form (frobenius_pullback, the formula after
that algorithm). Two cases occur:
  - the q-power lift: tau = id, q_tau = q = #k(P), n_tau = 1 (also used for ramified K);
  - the p-power lift: tau the Frobenius automorphism of the unramified K = L_P, q_tau = p,
    n_tau = f_K; F_q^* = (F_tau^*)^(n_tau). tau is given by a map L -> L: a global
    automorphism of L (frobenius_aut) or its approximation modulo P^prec (frobenius_aut_local).

The computations take place in R = (O_L/P^N)[x]((z))[y] with z standing for r(x): elements
of O_L[x,1/r,y]/(Q) are kept "radix reduced", i.e. every coefficient of a power of z has
x-degree < deg(r) and y-degree < d. Here r is normalised to be monic (r/lc(r) = z of the
other files, the article's r_mon).

Notation and conventions: see "Conventions and notation" in README.md.
```

Other functions of `frobenius.m`:

* `getrings(OL, P, N)` -- The coefficient rings for the Frobenius lift at precision N.
* `radix_reduce(f, r)` -- The radix-r normal form of f: every coefficient polynomial a(x) of a power z^k with deg(a) >= deg(r) is divided by r, a = c*r + b, and the quotient c is moved to z^(k+1).
* `reduce_mod_Q(f, Q, r)` -- The remainder of f modulo Q (y-degree < deg_y(Q)), in radix-r normal form.
* `substitute_xq(f, xq, r)` -- f(x^(q_tau),y): the substitution x -> x^(q_tau) in an element f of R without powers of z.
* `pow_mod_Q(f, n, Q, r)` -- f^n in R/(z-r,Q), by binary exponentiation with reductions modulo Q (radix-r normal form).
* `twist_pol(f, tau)` -- The coefficient twist f^tau of a polynomial f over O_L or L (article: r^tau, Delta^tau).
* `twist_polxy(f, tau)` -- The coefficient twist f^tau of f in (O_)L[x][y] (article: Q^tau, s^tau);
* `twist_mat(A, tau)` -- The entrywise twist tau(A) of a matrix A over L (article: tau^k(A_tau));
* `frobenius_aut(L, P)` -- A global automorphism of L inducing the Frobenius automorphism tau of K = L_P, for the p-power lift.
* `frobenius_aut_local(L, P, prec)` -- The Frobenius automorphism tau of K = L_P, as a map L -> L modulo P^prec;
* `froblift(Q, P, qtau, N, r, Delta, s, W0 : tau := 0)` -- The matrix of the Frobenius lift F_tau^* on functions, w.r.t. the basis b^0/r, modulo P^N (article: the algorithm "Compute matrix of Frobenius on functions").
* `frobenius_pullback(w, Q, P, qtau, N, r, frobmatb0r : tau := 0)` -- The pullback F_tau^*(omega) of omega = sum_i w_i b^0_i dx/r modulo P^N (article: the formula for the q_tau-power Frobenius of a differential form, after the algorithm "Compute matrix of Frobenius on functions").

## reduction.m

```text
reduction.m -- reduction of differential forms in cohomology, and reduction modulo P^N.

Article: section "Computing a basis of H^1_dR(U)": the proposition on the reduction of a
cohomology class and the algorithm "Reduction procedure", which writes a form as
    omega = d(f_0) + d(f_inf) + d(f_end) + sum_i a_i*omega_i
with omega_i the basis of basis_coho: the finite reduction (coho_red_fin) lowers the pole
order along the zeros of r, the infinite reduction (coho_red_inf) the pole order at the
points above infinity, and the final reduction (in reduce_with_fs) expresses the remainder
in E_0 cap E_inf in the basis. red_lists precomputes the matrices of the reduction steps;
the precision bounds are in the subsection "Precision bounds for reduction" and in the
proposition on the precision of the reduction process (section "Precision bounds").

Forms are coefficient vectors w of sum_i w_i b^0_i dx/z (resp. b^inf_i), z = r/lc(r); the
matrices act on coefficient rows from the right (the transposes of the article's).
The functions reduce_mod_PN_* replace an element of L (or of a polynomial ring, a series
ring or a matrix ring over L) by a simpler element congruent to it modulo P^N; this keeps
the coefficients of the exact arithmetic over L small.

Notation and conventions: see "Conventions and notation" in README.md.
```

Other functions of `reduction.m`:

* `reduce_mod_PN_L(f, L, P, N)` -- A simple element of L congruent to f modulo P^N (absolute precision N), i.e. f as an element of L_P modulo P^N.
* `mod_PN_data(denom, L, P, N)` -- For an integer denom with v = v_P(denom) and pi = UniformizingElement(P) (pi = p for a rational prime P = p).
* `reduce_mod_PN_Lx_cached(f, cache, L, P, N)` -- A simple polynomial in L[x] congruent to f in L[x] coefficientwise modulo P^N, and the associative array cache of the data of mod_PN_data (indexed by the denominator) with the entry used for f added.
* `reduce_mod_PN_Lx(f, L, P, N)` -- A simple polynomial in L[x] congruent to f in L[x] coefficientwise modulo P^N (reduce_mod_PN_Lx_cached without a cache).
* `reduce_mod_PN_L_mat(A, L, P, N)` -- A simple matrix over L congruent to A entrywise modulo P^N (as reduce_mod_PN_Lx).
* `reduce_mod_PN_Lttinv(f, L, P, N)` -- A simple Laurent series over L congruent to f coefficientwise modulo P^N (as reduce_mod_PN_Lx, applied to the coefficients of f in L((t))).
* `red_lists(Q, L, P, q, N, r, W0, Winf, G0, Ginf, e0, einf, J0, Jinf, T0, Tinf, T0inv, Tinfinv, eK, eKfK : qtau := 0)` -- The matrices of all steps of the finite and the infinite reduction of the Frobenius pullbacks F_tau^*(omega_i), computed once and shared by the 2g basis forms (article: the remark after the algorithm "Reduction procedure").
* `infinite_reduction_matrices(Ginf, mmax)` -- The matrices (Ginf_(-1) - m*I)^(-1), m = 1, ..., mmax, of the steps of the infinite reduction (red_list_inf of red_lists), Ginf_(-1) the residue matrix of Ginf at infinity.

* `substitute_z(f, g, L, P, N)` -- f(g): the substitution z -> g in f in L[x]((z)) without negative powers of z, exactly over L (the arguments L, P, N are not used).

## coercion.m

```text
coercion.m -- moving polynomials, series and matrices between L and its completion K.

The curve and the cohomological data live over the number field L; points, local
expansions and integrals live over the completion K = L_P (precision NK = Nmax) or over a
finite extension of K (the auxiliary totally ramified K(pi^(1/e)), or an unramified
extension). m : L -> K is the completion map (data`m), possibly composed with a power of the

Oq = O_K/pi^NK (series_K_to_Oq etc.), used by the tiny integrals and the local expansions.

Notation and conventions: see "Conventions and notation" in README.md.
```

## integrals.m

```text
integrals.m -- Coleman integrals on a curve over a finite extension K of Q_p.

Article: section "Computing Coleman integrals", with the bounds of section "Precision
bounds". The main functions:
  coleman_data(Q,L,P,N)         the Frobenius structure: the basis of H^1_dR(X), the matrix
                                A_q of F_q^* on it and the correction functions (the
                                algorithm "Matrices of F_tau^* and F_q^* on cohomology");
  set_point, set_bad_point      points (the point record is described at set_bad_point);
  tiny_integrals_on_basis       integrals of the basis forms within a residue disc (the
                                algorithm "Tiny integrals");
  coleman_integrals_on_basis    integrals of the basis forms between any two points (the
                                algorithm "Non-tiny integrals on basis");
  coleman_integral              the integral of an arbitrary form of the second kind (the
                                algorithm "Integrals in general").
A point is given by x (1/x at infinite points) and the values of the integral basis b^0
(resp. b^inf): (x,y) does not determine a point where the plane model is singular.

Conventions (see "Conventions and notation" in README.md):
  L the number field of the model Q, P a prime ideal of O_L, K = L_P the completion (with
  precision Nmax), m = data`m : L -> K; points and integrals live over K, or over the
  auxiliary totally ramified extension Ke = K(pi^(1/e)) used at bad endpoints;
  N the target precision of the integrals and Nmax the working precision, both absolute
  and in units of v_K (v_K(pi) = 1);
  forms are coefficient vectors w.r.t. b^0 dx/z with z = r/lc(r), and matrices act on such
  rows from the right (the transposes of the article's matrices);
  the Frobenius lift F_tau^* has exponent q_tau = data`qtau and coefficient automorphism
  tau = data`tau, and F_q^* = (F_tau^*)^(n_tau) with n_tau = data`ntau.
```

### coleman_data

```magma
coleman_data(Q, L, P, N : useU := false, basis0 := [], basis1 := [], basis2 := [], verbose := true, W0 := 0, Winf := 0, lift := "auto", full_check := false)
```

```text
The data for Coleman integration on the smooth projective curve X with plane model Q at
the completion K = L_P, to P-adic precision N (article: sections "Good models" to
"Computing Coleman integrals"; the steps are marked below).
Input:   Q in O_L[x][y], monic and irreducible in y, with good reduction at P in the sense
         of the definition "Good-reduction assumptions" (the cheap ones are tested, all of
         them with full_check); L a number field (or Q);
         P a prime ideal of O_L; N >= 1 the target precision, in units of v_K. Other
         forms of the input are accepted as described at coleman_data_input.
         Optional: useU (a basis of H^1_dR(U) instead of H^1_dR(X)); basis0, basis1, basis2
         (prescribed bases, see basis_coho); verbose (progress, timings and the sanity
         checks below); W0, Winf (the integral bases, as in mat_W0, mat_Winf; computed if
         0; for a model with rational coefficients they must have rational coefficients
         too, otherwise the model is treated over L);
         lift = "auto"/"p"/"q"/"plocal": the Frobenius lift. For f_K >= 2 the p-power
         lift (q_tau = p, n_tau = f_K) is used when possible ("auto"), which shrinks all
         series lengths by a factor of about q/p compared to the q-power lift; "q" forces
         the q-power lift, "p" fails if the p-power lift is not available. It is
         available if the model has rational coefficients, and otherwise whenever P is
         unramified: tau is then a global automorphism of L if there is one (e.g. L/Q

         P^Nmax (frobenius_aut_local). "plocal" is "p" with the latter forced (for tests);

         function field computations over F_q.
Returns: the record data described before its definition at the end of this function.
Errors:  if the input is invalid (coleman_data_input), or P fails one of the tested
```

### coleman_integrals_on_basis

```magma
coleman_integrals_on_basis(P1, P2, data : e := 0, verbose := true, side1 := 0, return_side := false, direct_inf := false, target := 0, warn := true)
```

```text
The Coleman integrals of the basis forms from P1 to P2 (algorithm "Non-tiny integrals on
basis").

Input:  P1, P2  points over K (set_point, set_bad_point): points of U, bad points or
                infinite points; a pair [x0,y0] stands for set_point(x0,y0,data),
        data    the output of coleman_data.
Output: the vector (int_P1^P2 omega_i)_i over K, i = 1..2g (the basis data`basis), and
        its absolute precision Nout (an integer, a lower bound for the number of correct
        digits of every entry).

Options:
  e          the ramification index of the auxiliary totally ramified extension
             K(pi^(1/e)) of the points near the boundary. For an endpoint in a bad
             residue disc with ramification index eP (of x at the very bad point of the
             disc) the point S near the boundary has v_K(r(S)) = eP/e, and correctness
             requires e > q*eP. Default (e = 0): e = 2*q*eP+1, which is run-time optimal
             (it keeps Nmax about 2*N instead of about (q*eP+1)*N for e = q*eP+1).
  verbose    print progress and the timings of the individual steps.
  return_side, side1
             with return_side := true the part of the computation that depends on P1
             only is returned as a third value; passed back as side1 it is reused for
             further integrals from the same base point P1 (as in the solver), which
             roughly halves their cost. The cached side fixes e and Ke.
  direct_inf use infinite endpoints without the extension K(pi^(1/e))
             (coleman_side_direct): an infinite point that is not very infinite is
             suitable, a very infinite one is replaced by the K-rational point t = pi of
             its disc. This saves the auxiliary extension but loses about q*(m-1) digits,
             m the pole order of the basis forms at the point at infinity; q-power lift
             only.
  target     the precision wanted (default 0: none): if the result is less precise and
             an endpoint uses a point near the boundary over K(pi^(1/e)), whose
             evaluation loses about j_0*e_P/e digits (see the note printed for e), the
             integrals are recomputed with e = 2e+1, up to e = (j_0-1)*e_P+1, beyond which
             a larger e gains nothing; otherwise a larger N in coleman_data is needed.
  warn       print the warning that p divides #J of the reduction, so that the linear
             solve loses digits (det(F-I) has positive valuation); false for a caller
             that computes many integrals with the same data and warns once itself.

Method: a bad endpoint that is not very bad is first replaced by the very bad point of
its disc (plus a tiny integral). Each endpoint P_j gives a point S_j (P_j itself if good,
a suitable point near the boundary otherwise; coleman_side), and the integrals J from S1
to S2 solve the Frobenius equation (matrices of F_tau^* and F_q^* on cohomology)
  (Phi - I) J = sum_k C_k (d_k(S1) - d_k(S2)) - tiny(S1 -> F_q S1) + tiny(S2 -> F_q S2),
where Phi = data`F, C_k = data`Clist[k+1] and d_k are the values of the twisted primitives
f_{tau,i} at F_tau^k(S_j), k = 0..ntau-1 (for the q-power lift: ntau = 1 and C_0 = I).
Finally int_P1^P2 = tiny(P1 -> S1) + J - tiny(P2 -> S2), rounded from Ke to K.
```

### coleman_integral

```magma
coleman_integral(P1, P2, dif, data : IP1P2 := 0, NIP1P2 := 0, direct_inf := false)
```

```text
The Coleman integral of a 1-form from P1 to P2 (algorithm "Integrals in general"): of the
second kind, or, with data for a basis of H^1_dR(U) (coleman_data(...: useU := true)), of
the third kind with poles at points of X - U (the points above the zeros of r and above
x = infinity); the logarithms are on Iwasawa's branch log(p) = 0 (log_iwasawa).

Input:  P1, P2  points over K that are not very bad (the primitives f_0, f_inf, f_end of
                dif can have poles at the very bad points); a pair [x0,y0] stands for
                set_point(x0,y0,data),
        dif     the form sum_i dif[i]*b^0_i dx/z, z = r/lc(r): a sequence or vector of d
                polynomials in x over L (or over Q), like the entries of data`basis (of
                the second kind on U, or of the third kind as above),
        data    the output of coleman_data.
Output: int_P1^P2 dif in K and its absolute precision.

Options: IP1P2, NIP1P2  the integrals of the basis forms from P1 to P2 and their
         precision (coleman_integrals_on_basis), if already known; direct_inf as there.

Method: reduce_with_fs writes dif = d(f_0) + d(f_inf) + d(f_end) + sum_i c_i omega_i, so
  int_P1^P2 dif = f(P2) - f(P1) + sum_i c_i int_P1^P2 omega_i,  f = f_0 + f_inf + f_end.
The reduction runs over the field of the reduction data: L, or Q in the rational branch
of coleman_data, where a form with coefficients in L is split into its rational
coordinates w.r.t. the power basis of L.
Precision: dif has at most simple poles above the zeros of r, so the finite reduction
does nothing (f_0 = 0). The reduction runs modulo pi^Nmax; by the propositions on the
infinite and on the final reduction it loses e_K*floor(log_p(mu*e_inf)) + c_end digits,
mu = -ord_inf(w) - deg(r) + 1 for the coefficients w of dif w.r.t. b^inf (and for
coefficients of valuation v < 0 the form pi^(-v)*dif is the integral one). So c_i,
f_inf and f_end are correct to the precision Ncoef = Nmax + min(v,0) - loss, and their
values at the points are evaluated with Ncoef in place of N if it is smaller.
```

```text
known points, algebraic recognition, a Mordell-Weil sieve at a second prime, or a larger
N). Over a ramified K the coefficients pi^j*c_j of h_i can have negative valuation (the
denominators j of the integration exceed j*v(pi) for small p and large e_K); h_i is
then divided by its content.
```

### ros_chabauty

```magma
ros_chabauty(datas, known : e := 0, htbound := 10^4)
```

```text
Chabauty-Coleman for the restriction of scalars (Siksek, "Explicit Chabauty over number
fields"): the L-rational points of the curve from known points, with the completions
K_1, ..., K_k of L at several primes P_1, ..., P_k above p at once, as a Q_p-analytic
problem. This needs rank <= (g-1)*n, n = sum_j [K_j:Q_p], instead of rank < g for a
single completion (effective_chabauty).

Input:  datas    the outputs of coleman_data for the same model (the same polynomial Q)
                 over the same number field L at distinct primes above the same p (e.g.
                 all of them);
        known    the known L-rational points, at least two: pairs [x0, y0] of coordinates
                 of good points (set_point at every prime), or lists [* R_1, ..., R_k *] of
                 the point records of a point at the primes of datas;
        e        as in coleman_integrals_on_basis;
        htbound  the height bound of recognize_in_F, for new points.
Output: a record with
          functionals  the vanishing functionals, the rows of a matrix over Q_p: Q_p-linear
                       forms in the integrals of the holomorphic forms omega_1, ...,
                       omega_g of data`basis at all primes (the columns: the coordinates
                       qp_coordinates of int omega_i at P_j, ordered by j, then i) that
                       vanish on the integrals between the known points,
          discs        for every prime the centres of its residue discs (Qq_points),
          polydiscs    for every residue polydisc D_1 x ... x D_k (a residue disc at every
                       prime) a record with the fields
                         discs   the indices of the D_j in discs,
                         known   the indices of the known points in it,
                         status  "known": it contains exactly one L-rational point, the
                                 known one; "empty": none; "one": the functionals have
                                 exactly one common zero in it, not a known point, whose
                                 approximations at the primes are in points, and point =
                                 [x0, y0] if it is recognized as an L-rational point
                                 (else []); "unresolved": none of these could be shown,
          new          the recognized L-rational points [x0, y0] that are not known,
          complete     whether every polydisc is "known", "empty", or "one" with a
                       recognized point: then the L-rational points are the known and the
                       new ones.
The conclusions about L-rational points hold if the known points generate a subgroup of
finite index of the Mordell-Weil group of the Jacobian over L. A polydisc "one" with an
unrecognized zero needs another argument (e.g. a Mordell-Weil sieve); for rank exactly
(g-1)*n this is the typical case of the polydiscs without known points.

Method. The integrals int_Q1^Q omega at the primes P_j (Q1 = known[1]) of an L-rational
point Q lie in K_1^g x ... x K_k^g, a Q_p-space of dimension g*n, and those of the known
points span a subspace of dimension r (the rank); the vanishing functionals are its
annihilator, of dimension kappa = g*n - r >= n (the left kernel of the matrix of the
Q_p-coordinates, basis_kernel). On a polydisc with centres T_j (Qq_points) and local
parameters z_j = pi_j*sum_b t_jb*theta_jb (theta_j the Z_p-basis qp_basis(K_j) of O_{K_j},
t in Z_p^n), the functionals of int_Q1^T_j omega + tiny(T_j -> z_j) are power series
  F(t) = c + A*t + H(t),   H of order >= 2.
If A has a left inverse B over Q_p (ros_left_inverse) with all coefficients of B*H in
p*Z_p, then t -> -B*c - B*H(t) is a contraction of Z_p^n (a form of Siksek's criterion):
B*F has no zero in Z_p^n if B*c is not integral and exactly one otherwise, found by the
iteration t -> t - B*F(t); if kappa > n, the common zeros of F are among it, and none if
F does not vanish there. The coefficient of t^alpha (|alpha| = m) in z_j^m is
pi_j^m*gamma_alpha with gamma_alpha in O_{K_j}, a Z_p-combination of the theta_jb, so the
coefficients of B*H are bounded by the values of B*lambda on s_m*pi_j^m*theta_jb (s_m the
coefficients of the tiny integrals); the omitted tail and the errors of the values are
below p^(Nw-delta), where v(B) >= -delta and Nw is the precision of the values: that of
the integrals, and that of the functionals (basis_kernel: the precision of the
integrals between the known points minus the valuation of the largest elementary
divisor of their matrix). If the criterion fails, a polydisc without known points is
still empty if a functional has a constant term of smaller valuation than all its other
coefficients.
```

## symmetric_chabauty.m

```text
symmetric_chabauty.m

Siksek symmetric Chabauty for degree-2 (quadratic) points, built on the
Cai-Keller Coleman-integration + effective-Chabauty code (integrals.m, solver.m).
Load AFTER integrals.m and solver.m.

Target use case: a curve C/F over a number field F, find the quadratic points
X^{(2)}(F) (= effective F-rational degree-2 divisors) at a prime P of F, given
rank J(F) <= g-2. In the rank-0 case the annihilating space V is the full space
of holomorphic differentials and no Mordell-Weil integration is needed.

The local criterion (Siksek, "Chabauty for symmetric powers of curves", Thm 3.2):
for a residue disc-pair carrying a known degree-2 divisor Q_1+Q_2, the matrix of
leading Taylor coefficients of the annihilating differentials, reduced mod P, has
rank 2  ==>  that disc-pair contains AT MOST ONE F-rational quadratic point.
  split  (Q_1 != Q_2 mod P):  column_i = ( a_0(Q_i, omega_j) )_j
  diagonal (Q_1 = Q_2 = Q):   columns   = ( a_0(Q,omega_j) )_j , ( -a_1(Q,omega_j) )_j
where a_l(Q,omega) is the l-th Taylor coefficient of omega/dt at Q.

TWO SPACES OF DIFFERENTIALS.  The
rank-2 test above may use ANY subspace V of the annihilator of J(F), presented by
a SATURATED (integral) basis -- see differentials_are_saturated.  The rank-DROP
branch is a different statement and needs a different space: the conclusion
"every degree-2 point of this class is a pullback rho^*(P) along a degree-2 map
rho : X -> C'" holds only
  (i)  for classes that are themselves of pullback type, Qbar + sigma(Qbar), and
  (ii) using TRACE-ZERO differentials, W \subseteq V \cap ker(rho_*) =
       V \cap H^0(X,Omega)^{sigma^* = -1}  (the Prym differentials),
because only for those is the whole pullback family contained in the zero locus
of the Chabauty equations (see the proof sketch at symmetric_chabauty_classify).
A rank-1 class that is NOT of pullback type ("cross" class: two points with the
same image under X -> P(W^*), but not sigma-conjugate) is certified by NOTHING at
first order.  So a rank-1 class must not be labelled "family/pullback-certified"
in general: at q = 41 on X(s3,ns5), 32 of the 74 rank-1 classes are cross classes,
not family classes.  symmetric_chabauty_classify returns them separately, in `cross`.

Status: the criterion is implemented for all three disc-pair kinds -- split,
diagonal, and Frobenius-conjugate (degree-2 closed points of X/F_q) -- for any
genus g and any annihilating space V with dim V >= 2. The criterion matrices use
ALL differentials of V as rows (any subspace of the annihilating space is
admissible in Siksek's rank-2 criterion; extra rows can only increase the rank,
i.e. certify more disc-pairs; for dim V = 2, as in every run for the paper, this
is the same as using the first two differentials). Recovery of the
quadratic points is implemented for split disc-pairs (2x2 Newton in the two disc
parameters + LLL recognition over O_F + rigorous on-curve verification), for diagonal
disc-pairs (Newton in the symmetric functions t1+t2, t1*t2 of the disc parameters,
newton_symmetric; the Jacobian in (t1,t2) is singular on the diagonal), and, with the
option include_bad of quadratic_points_split, in the finite bad discs (expanded at their
very bad point). Frobenius-conjugate disc-pairs (degree-2 places of X/F_q) are split
disc-pairs over a K with f_K = 2 (run over such a K); the infinite discs are not covered.
What is not recovered is COUNTED explicitly, never dropped silently.

Current restrictions of this layer (the integration layer below is more general):
 (1) P unramified (e_P = 1): the LLL recognition (recognize_in_F) assumes it.
 (2) Any residue degree f(P) is allowed: the LLL
     recognition runs against the f_P Q_p-coordinates of an element of
     K = Q_{p^{f_P}} coordinatewise, so it works for any residue degree; the
     target order can be supplied via the `basis` parameter (use O_F when the
     value is known to be Galois-fixed, which keeps the lattice small). This
     is what makes the Frobenius-conjugate disc-pairs recoverable: see the
     note at quadratic_points_split.
 (3) Qq_points (solver.m), used here to enumerate disc centres, reduces the
     model through L -> K -> F_q, so the model may have arbitrary coefficients in
     L, as in the integration layer (tests/examples/quadratic-points-over-F.m:
     curves genuinely defined over Q(sqrt 5)).
 (4) The PULLBACK branch needs the involution sigma as a 3 x 3 matrix on the
     plane model and the caller's assertion trace_zero (that W consists of
     trace-zero differentials for that sigma).  Without them no class is ever
     labelled pullback and every rank-drop class is reported as uncertified --
     the conservative default.
Validated configurations: g = 2, rank 0, V = full holomorphic space
(X(b3,ns5)/Q(sqrt(5)) at P | 11, test-symchab-Xb3ns5.m), and g = 3, rank 1,
dim V = 2 from vanishing_differentials (X(s3,ns5)/Q(sqrt(5)) at q = 19, 41, 79, 89;
test-symchab-Xs3ns5.m).  For X(s3,ns5) the cross classes are disposed of by the
Mordell-Weil sieve of appendix-A-sieve.m.
curve_quadratic_points (quadratic_points.m) applies this layer to every residue class
of a curve given as a Curve, the infinite and bad discs included (in patches of the
model), and reports the status of every class.
```

### symmetric_chabauty_classify

```magma
symmetric_chabauty_classify(data, V : prec := 0, verbose := true, sigma := 0, trace_zero := false, W := [])
```

```text
General classifier: given annihilating differentials V (a list of >= 2 length-g
coefficient vectors against the holomorphic basis, e.g. from
vanishing_differentials, or annihilating_differentials_full at rank 0), apply
the d=2 Siksek criterion to every residue disc-pair (split, diagonal and
Frobenius-conjugate).  V must be a SATURATED (integral) basis; it is tested and
repaired if not (see differentials_are_saturated / saturate_differentials).

THREE outcomes per residue class (a two-way split "rank 2 / rank 1 = family"
would not be sound):

  CERTIFIED  rk(Abar_V) = 2 : the class contains at most one point of X^(2)(F).
             (Siksek; valid for any V inside the annihilator of J(F).)

  PULLBACK   the class is itself of pullback type, Qbar + sigma(Qbar), AND the
             differentials used have TRACE ZERO, i.e. lie in ker(rho_*) =
             H^0(X,Omega)^{sigma^* = -1}, AND rk(Abar_W) >= 1 : then every point
             of X^(2)(F) in the class is a pullback rho^*(P), P in C'(F).
             Reason: for a trace-zero omega and D_0 = rho^*(pt), transporting the
             disc parameter by sigma gives f_omega(t_1,t_2) = g(t_1) - g(t_2),
             which factors as (t_1-t_2)*h with h(0,0) = a_0(Qbar,omega); if some
             omega in W has a_0 a unit then h is a unit on the whole polydisc
             (p > 2), so t_1 = t_2, i.e. the point is rho^*(P).  The SAME
             argument, with the parameter at a ramification point normalised so
             that sigma(t) = -t, covers the diagonal class 2Qbar at a fixed point
             of sigma, and it covers a conjugate class {Q,Q^(q)} with
             sigma(Q) = Q^(q) after base change to the unramified quadratic
             extension, where that class becomes a split one.
             NB the trace-zero hypothesis is essential: for omega in the full
             annihilator that is NOT trace-zero, the pullback family is not
             contained in the zero locus of f_omega and the factorisation fails.

  UNCERTIFIED everything else.  In particular rk(Abar) = 1 on a class that is NOT
             of pullback type ("CROSS" classes: two points with the same image
             under the map X -> P(W^*) defined by W, but not sigma-conjugate).
             These are NOT family classes and nothing is proved about them here;
             they are returned explicitly in `cross` so that they can be disposed
             of by other means (a Mordell-Weil / reachability argument, or a
             second prime).  For C = X(s3,ns5) there are 32 of them at q = 41
             against 42 genuine pullback classes.

Optional parameters:
  sigma      3 x 3 matrix over data`L (or Q) giving the involution on the plane
             model, column convention (x:y:z) |-> sigma*(x,y,z)^t.  Without it no
             class is ever labelled PULLBACK: rank-drop classes are all reported
             as uncertified.
  trace_zero assert that the differentials of W are trace zero for that sigma.
             Required for the PULLBACK conclusion.  (For X(s3,ns5) it holds for
             a structural reason: J ~ E x A with rk A(F) = 0, so the 2-dimensional
             Prym = trace-zero space is annihilating and already exhausts
             dim V = g - rk J(F) = 2, whence V = W is the trace-zero space.)
  W          the trace-zero subspace to use in the PULLBACK test; defaults to V.

Returns a record with the counts and the classified lists.
```

## models.m

```text
models.m -- plane models of a curve for the Coleman integration code: the good primes of a
model, better models, the affine patches of a curve, its points as point records, and
Chabauty-Coleman for a curve given as a Curve.

The library needs a plane model Q(x,y) = 0, monic in y with integral coefficients, and a
prime P at which the triple (Q, W^0, W^inf) has good reduction (article: the definition
"Good-reduction assumptions"; README: "Conventions and notation"). The functions below
produce such models from a polynomial or from a Curve, find their good primes, and convert
the points of the curve into point records:

  curve_model(C)               a model of C: a Curve in affine or projective space (projected
                               to a plane curve if necessary), or a polynomial Q(x,y);
  good_primes(M)               the primes of small norm at which the model M has good
                               reduction, and the reason for every other prime;
  improve_model(M)             models obtained from M by transformations that preserve the
                               curve, ranked by their good primes;
  model_coleman_data(M, P, N)  coleman_data for the model M, also (aux) over the unramified
                               quadratic extension of L_P, for the points of degree 2;
  affine_patches(M, P)         plane models (affine patches of the plane curve) whose good
                               residue discs at P cover all smooth points of the reduction;
  point_records(M, data, pts)  the point records of points of the curve (also of places of
                               degree 2 and of divisors), for data computed by coleman_data
                               from the model;
  curve_chabauty(C, pts)       Chabauty-Coleman for a curve given as a Curve or a polynomial:
                               model, prime, coleman_data, effective_chabauty, and the
                               identification of the candidates.

A model is a record with the fields
  Q       the plane model in L[x][y], monic in y with integral coefficients,
  L       the number field of the model (Q as a number field of degree 1 for a curve over Q),
  C       the curve, FF its function field (over the base field K of C),
  xf, yf  the functions x and y of the model, elements of FF with Q(xf,yf) = 0,
  F       the homogeneous equation over L of a plane curve birational to C, in the
          coordinates (u_1 : u_2 : u_3) = base,
  base    functions u_1, u_2, u_3 in FF with F(u_1,u_2,u_3) = 0,
  c       the polynomial c(x) over L with yf = c(xf)*u_2/u_3 (and xf = u_1/u_3),
  desc    the transformations that led to the model (a sequence of strings).
Every point of the smooth projective curve -- also a point at infinity of the plane model
or a singular point of it -- is a place of FF, so point_records converts all points of C,
whatever model is used. quadratic_points.m builds on these functions for the points of
degree 2 (curve_quadratic_points).

Load after integrals.m (which contains the tests of the good-reduction assumptions,
good_reduction_at) and, for curve_chabauty, after solver.m.
```

```text
reduction of the model; preferably p dividing #J(F_q), see "excluded" in (5)):
 (1) Residue classes. An F-rational effective divisor D of degree 2 reduces to a divisor of
     degree 2 of the reduction over the residue field F_q of P: 2*Pb or Pb + Qb with Pb, Qb
     in X(F_q), or a closed point of degree 2, whose two points are Frobenius-conjugate
     points of X(F_(q^2)). Over the unramified quadratic extension K of F_P
     (model_coleman_data with aux: the completion of L2 = F(sqrt u) at the prime above P,
     u a non-square modulo P) these are the pairs {a, b} of residue discs over K that are
     stable under the Frobenius x -> x^q of F_(q^2)/F_q: split classes {a, b} of two
     F_q-rational discs, diagonal classes {a, a}, and conjugate classes {a, Frob(a)}.
     Without aux only the split and diagonal classes are treated.
 (2) Residue discs. Every point of the reduction over the residue field of K is the centre
     of a disc in a model in which it is good (its home): the finite good discs of the
     model M; for a model of degree 2 in y (y^2 + h(x)*y = f(x)) the discs at infinity in
     two Moebius patches x = t + 1/x' (mobius_model) and the Weierstrass discs in M itself,
     centred at their Weierstrass point, which is a very bad point of the patches as well;
     for the other models the infinite and finite bad discs at smooth points of the reduced
     plane curve in linear patches where they are good (affine_patches), further linear
     patches for the pairs of discs that are good in no common model (cover_classes), and
     the bad discs at singular points of the reduced plane curve in M itself, centred at
     their very bad point (include_bad; their integrals need the auxiliary extension of
     integrals.m); discs at singular points at infinity are not covered.
 (3) Integrals. For a base point B0 (the centre of the first good disc of M) the integrals
     J = int_(B0)^(c) of the holomorphic forms omega_1..omega_g of M to every centre c,
     computed in the home model of the disc: through a point that is good in both models
     and the transition matrix of holomorphic_transition. For a Weierstrass point W of a
     model of degree 2 in y, int_(B0)^(W) = 1/2 int_(B0)^(iota(B0)) with the hyperelliptic
     involution iota (it acts by -1 on the holomorphic forms; W is fixed), with no
     auxiliary extension. The integral to a point of a disc is J of its centre plus a tiny
     integral.
 (4) The annihilating differentials V: the holomorphic forms whose integrals vanish on the
     differences D - D0 of the known divisors (D0 the first known divisor whose points are
     defined over K) and on the given generators; a saturated basis (basis_kernel). If the
     known divisors and the generators generate a subgroup of finite index of J(F), the
     integrals of all omega in V vanish on every F-rational divisor of degree 0, and dim V
     >= g - rank J(F); symmetric Chabauty needs dim V >= 2.
 (5) Every class {a, b} is treated in a model h in which both discs are good (or a bad disc
     has its very bad point). The integrals I_k(t1,t2) = int_(D0)^(D(t1,t2)) omega_k, omega_k in
     V, of the divisors D(t1,t2) of the class (t1, t2 the parameters of the two discs) are
     I_k(0,0) plus the tiny integrals in the two discs:
       excluded   some I_k(0,0) is a unit: no divisor of the class is a zero of I_k, so the
                  class contains no F-rational divisor (possible only if p divides #J(F_q):
                  the class contains an F_P-rational divisor D, and I_k(0,0) is congruent
                  modulo P to the logarithm of [D - D0] in J(F_P));
       certified  the linear terms of two of the forms have rank 2 modulo P (Siksek's
                  criterion; for a diagonal class the jet (a_0, a_1) at the centre): the
                  class contains at most one zero of the two, found by Newton's method in
                  (t1, t2) (in (t1+t2, t1*t2) for a diagonal class, newton_symmetric);
                  a known divisor in a certified class is its only F-rational divisor
                  (known); otherwise the class is excluded if another form of V does not
                  vanish at the zero, and else the symmetric functions of x at the zero are
                  recognized as elements of F of small height (recognize_in_F) and verified:
                  a divisor over F with these values of x whose points lie in the two discs
                  is the only F-rational divisor of the class (recovered; a point of degree
                  2 ramified at P has no points over K and lies in the diagonal class of its
                  reduction, which place_key computes);
       family     rank < 2 on a class of pullback type Qb + iota(Qb) (iota the
                  hyperelliptic involution for a model of degree 2 in y, or sigma) with a
                  unit linear term: every F-rational divisor of the class is a pullback
                  (trace-zero differentials, see symmetric_chabauty_classify);
       unresolved everything else (rank drop, a zero that is not recognized or not verified:
                  a value of small height that is no divisor in the class is a spurious match
                  of the recognition, for lack of precision).
The result is complete if no class is unresolved and every disc is covered: then the
F-rational effective divisors of degree 2 are the known and the recovered ones and the
pullbacks in the family classes -- provided that V annihilates J(F) (the known divisors and
the generators generate a subgroup of finite index of J(F); this is not checked). For
dim V = 2 (rank J(F) = g - 2, or g = 2 and rank 0) every certified class that is not
excluded has a p-adic zero, so a single prime gives a complete result only if p divides
#J(F_q) (where unit integrals exclude classes) or with a Mordell-Weil sieve (the option
reachable); for dim V >= 3 the further forms exclude most classes.

Load after symmetric_chabauty.m and models.m.
```

### curve_quadratic_points

```magma
curve_quadratic_points(C, known : P := 0, N := 15, bound := 50, improve := true, generators := [], aux := true, include_bad := true, htbound := 10^5, sigma := 0, trace_zero := false, reachable := 0, primes := 1, minp := 5, rank_check := false, verbose := true)
```

```text
The F-rational effective divisors of degree 2 (the quadratic points and the sums of two
F-rational points) of a curve C over a number field F (or Q), by symmetric Chabauty from
known ones (see the file header for the method and the meaning of the result).
Input:  C a Curve or a polynomial (curve_model), or a model; known the known F-rational
        effective divisors of degree 2, each an effective divisor of C, a place of degree
        2, a point over a quadratic extension of F, or a pair of points of C(F) (or places
        of degree 1, or pairs [x0, y0] of coordinates of the model); at least one of them
        must have its points defined over K (over the residue field of K: not ramified at
        P), e.g. P1 + P2 for two F-rational points, as the base divisor D0.
Options:
  P          the prime of F (default: a good prime of residue characteristic >= minp that
             is unramified over p: one with p dividing #J(F_q) if there is one, since only
             there can a class be excluded by a unit integral (for dim V = 2 a single prime
             can only give a complete result there); the smallest norm among them, since
             the cost grows about like q^3);
  N          the precision of coleman_data (the recognition of the zeros needs a few
             digits more than the height of the divisors);
  bound      the bound for the norms of the primes tested (good_primes);
  improve    choose the model by improve_model (otherwise curve_model; not with sigma,
             which refers to the plane coordinates of curve_model);
  generators pairs [D1, D2] of effective divisors (in any of the forms of known) of the
             same degree whose differences, with those of the known divisors, generate a
             subgroup of finite index of J(F) (the differences D - D0 of the known divisors
             are used in any case);
  aux        compute over the unramified quadratic extension of F_P, which covers the
             classes of the points of degree 2 of the reduction (default true);
  include_bad  treat the bad discs at singular points of the reduced plane curve (for a
             model of degree >= 3 in y; their integrals need the auxiliary extension);
  htbound    the height bound of the recognition (recognize_in_F);
  sigma, trace_zero  an involution of the curve as a 3 x 3 matrix on the plane
             coordinates of the model (of the plane curve of C for a Curve, of (x : y : 1)
             for a polynomial; column convention, as in symmetric_chabauty_classify)
             and the assertion that V consists of trace-zero differentials for it: rank-drop
             classes of pullback type are then classes of pullbacks (for a model of degree 2
             in y the hyperelliptic involution is used automatically);
  reachable  a function (class, discs) -> boolean, false if the class contains no
             F-rational divisor (e.g. a Mordell-Weil sieve: the class of the reduction is
             not in the reduction of J(F)); see class_format, disc_format;
  primes     the number of primes to use at most: a further one while the result is not
             complete, with the divisors recovered so far added to the known ones;
  minp       the least residue characteristic of the default primes (p >= 5 makes the
             diagonal classes certifiable through the jet (a_0, a_1));
  rank_check bounds for the rank of J(F) (jacobian_rank_bounds: models y^2 + h(x)*y = f(x)
             of genus 2, by 2-descent): the integrals of the known divisors and the
             generators have rank g - dim V, at most the rank of the subgroup they span, so
             an upper bound <= g - dim V shows that this subgroup has finite index in J(F)
             and V annihilates J(F) (res`annihilating); otherwise a warning;
  verbose    print the progress.
Output: a record of quadratic_result_format (the last prime), with primes the primes used
        and complete true if the result at one of them is complete.
```
